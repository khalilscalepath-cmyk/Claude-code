# Shorts cut from long video 001

Four Shorts, each 35 to 45 seconds of narration. Paste only the text under "Narration". Facts and sources are the same as in `001-earth-2-0.md`.

## Short 1: A birthday every six days

**On-screen hook text:** "On this planet you'd be 1,800 years old"

**Narration**

There is a planet where you would have a birthday every six days.

It's called TRAPPIST-1e, and it is the most Earth-like planet we know. Almost the same size as Earth. Almost the same starting temperature.

But its star is tiny and dim, so the planet has to huddle in close to stay warm. Thirty times closer than Earth is to the Sun.

That close, one lap around the star takes six days. That's a full year.

So if you are thirty on Earth, you'd be about eighteen hundred there.

The bad news? It is forty light-years away. Nobody is going to your party.

**Visuals:** Earth and TRAPPIST-1e side by side → tight orbit diagram → calendar flipping six pages → character behind a mountain of cakes → long empty line to a distant star.

## Short 2: How far is one light-day?

**On-screen hook text:** "49 years to travel ONE light-day"

**Narration**

Voyager 1 left Earth in nineteen seventy-seven.

It has been flying ever since at seventeen kilometres every second. Nothing we have sent out of the solar system has gone faster.

This month, after forty-nine years, it reaches one light-day from Earth. The distance light covers in a single day.

Now here is the problem. The nearest planet that looks anything like Earth is forty light-years away. That is more than fourteen thousand light-days.

At Voyager's speed, the trip takes over seven hundred thousand years.

Humans have only existed for three hundred thousand.

Space is not far. Space is absurd.

**Visuals:** Voyager launching, "1977" → speedometer "17 km/s" → ruler with one tick marked "1 light-day" → ruler stretches off screen to 14,000 ticks → two bars: 700,000 years vs 300,000 years.

## Short 3: How to smell a planet from 40 light-years away

**On-screen hook text:** "How do we know what alien air is made of?"

**Narration**

How do you find out what the air is made of on a planet you can't even see?

You wait for it to pass in front of its star.

When it does, a thin sliver of starlight shines through the planet's atmosphere on the way to us. And every gas swallows its own colours of light.

So you spread the starlight into a rainbow and look for the gaps. The missing colours are a fingerprint. Carbon dioxide leaves one pattern. Methane leaves another.

The change is tiny, a few thousandths of one percent. It is like spotting a gnat crossing a searchlight from another city.

Only one telescope can do it. And right now, it is pointed at the most Earth-like planet we know.

**Visuals:** dark planet silhouette crossing a star → light ray passing through a thin blue ring → rainbow bar with dark gaps labelled CO₂ and CH₄ → searchlight and gnat → cartoon Webb telescope.

## Short 4: The planet where the sun never sets

**On-screen hook text:** "One side is always day. One side is always night."

**Narration**

On this planet, the sun never rises and never sets.

TRAPPIST-1e orbits so close to its star that gravity has almost certainly locked it in place, the same way the Moon always shows Earth the same face.

One half of the planet is stuck in permanent afternoon. The other half is in a night that never ends.

So where would you live? Probably on the thin ring in between. A strip of endless sunset that runs all the way around the planet.

Above you, a red sun four times wider than ours, frozen in the sky. And six other planets drifting past, some looking bigger than our Moon.

Whether there is any air to breathe there, we still don't know.

**Visuals:** planet split into orange and dark blue halves → Moon and Earth comparison → twilight ring highlighted → landscape with fixed red sun and sister planets → black card: "Air? Still unknown."
